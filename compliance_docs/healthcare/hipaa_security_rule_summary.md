# HIPAA Security and Privacy Rules: summary of selected sections

Plain-English summary for PerenicAI demonstrations. Not the official text of
45 CFR Parts 160 and 164, and not legal advice. ePHI means electronic
protected health information.

## §164.312(a)(1) Access control

Systems that hold ePHI must only allow access to people and software that have
been granted access rights. Required parts include a unique identifier for
each user (§164.312(a)(2)(i)). Encrypting and decrypting ePHI is an
addressable safeguard (§164.312(a)(2)(iv)).

## §164.312(b) Audit controls

Put in place mechanisms that record and examine activity in systems that
contain or use ePHI, for example who viewed or changed a patient record and
when.

## §164.312(c)(1) Integrity

Protect ePHI from being improperly altered or destroyed.

## §164.312(d) Person or entity authentication

Verify that a person or system asking for access to ePHI is who they claim to
be.

## §164.312(e)(1) Transmission security

Guard against unauthorised access to ePHI while it is sent over an electronic
network. Encryption in transit is an addressable safeguard
(§164.312(e)(2)(ii)).

## §164.502(b) Minimum necessary

When using or disclosing PHI, limit it to the minimum needed for the purpose.
Copying whole patient records into logs, error messages or analytics when only
an ID is needed goes against this standard.

## §164.514(b) De-identification

Data counts as de-identified under the "safe harbor" method only when 18 kinds
of identifier are removed, including names, Social Security numbers, medical
record numbers, and all parts of dates (except the year) directly related to
the individual, such as date of birth.
